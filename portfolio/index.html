About me

My whole career has been about making interactive, real-time things — mostly games, sometimes tools that feel like them.

I started with C#, .NET and Unity, shipping mobile games, advergames and XR experiences for clients at Reply and live-ops titles at Miniclip. Since 2022 I've been working in C++ and Unreal Engine 5 at Zuru Tech, on the drawing tools of a BIM platform that designs buildings and sends them straight to an automated factory.

Juggling client projects at Reply, a live game at Miniclip and a large product codebase at Zuru Tech taught me as much about people as about code: I'm organized, dependable and happiest in a team that ships together.

Off the clock I play far too many games — mostly single-player adventures, RPGs, puzzlers and platformers — and run tabletop campaigns as a Dungeon Master. I also keep a steady stream of personal game projects going. Most never leave my hard drive, and I enjoy every one of them anyway.

How I code with AI

I was an experienced software engineer long before AI entered my workflow: years of building games and real-time 3D software by hand came first, and I only started working with AI in recent years.

Today I use coding agents every day: Claude Code, Codex and others. That background is exactly why they work for me. They are a real multiplier, but only in expert hands.

At Zuru Tech I was part of the AI Enabling team, which took up AI-assisted development before today's high-performing models and agents existed. Working within those limits taught us where these tools help, where they quietly mislead, and what it takes to get production-quality code out of them. Then we taught it to the other teams, one team at a time.

  1. Experts in charge

    The agent writes code; I own it. Architecture, trade-offs and the final call stay with someone who could have written it by hand.

  2. Reviewed like any other code

    Every change is read, questioned and tested before it lands. Generated code gets no free pass, and green tests alone don't make it right.

  3. Engineered, not just prompted

    Specs, conventions, schemas, tests and CI gates give the agent rails to run on, so good output is repeatable instead of lucky.

  4. Organized

    Small, reviewable steps, one source of truth for every rule, and documentation that people and agents both read and keep current.
